PRESENTATION STORY NOTES

Communication job
-----------------
By the end, Kian's friends should understand what the trip actually felt like
and why it mattered. The deck should turn 17 elapsed days into a lived sequence:
the plan breaks almost immediately, improvisation becomes the real route, and
the finish matters because people keep making the trip possible.

Narrative spine
---------------
1. Open at the finish, not the airport: Kian lifts the bicycle at night after
   3,049 km. Family is waiting just outside the frame.
2. Reveal the original 4,173 km loop and the 3,049 km route that actually happened.
   This is not a story about flawlessly executing a plan.
3. Day 1 nearly ends the trip: a loose pedal destroys the crank threads before
   Mainz; four shops later, the ride continues to Karlsruhe.
4. Heat, missing gear, a near-lost passport, and a four-country day accumulate.
   The Day 4 Alpine storm kills the phone and forces the first rest day.
5. Day 5 supplies the spectacular center: Oberalp and Furka. The emotional line
   is not triumph; it is Kian deciding to stop being angry and look forward.
6. Geneva is the low point. Two Airbnbs cancel, the backup phone dies, stores are
   closed, and Kian cries at the station. Zahran walks up and offers his balcony.
7. The phone gets repaired, but the original route does not. Completing it would
   require 264 km every day plus the Italian Alps, so Kian turns north at Lyon.
8. The bicycle immediately punctures. Kian dumps both water bottles, leans back,
   and hand-pumps every few kilometers to reach Olivier and Anton, who repair it
   and refuse labor payment.
9. The middle of France becomes deliberate attrition: 275 km to Nevers, then 287
   km to Paris. The scenery remains beautiful, but the body and route are messy.
10. Paris and Drocourt are not detours from the story. His aunt, Jean-Luc, and a
    day off restore the human scale before the final push.
11. Belgium supplies cobbles, headwind, Ghent's infrastructure, and Bruges. With
    600 km left, Kian chooses two enormous days because home now matters more than
    preserving a sensible schedule.
12. Day 13 is 337.84 km, the longest ride of his life. Day 14 is another 270.42 km
    on dead legs. The family tracks his phone and waits in the driveway after 11.
13. Close on the July 10 note, not a generic lesson: "I LOVE LIFE SO MUCH."

Recurring human pattern
-----------------------
The trip repeatedly survives because somebody helps: the fourth Mainz bike shop;
Jerome and Marika during the Danis rest day; Zahran in Geneva; the cousin who
transfers EUR 180; Olivier and Anton at Giant; the Airbnb host who offers food;
Kian's aunt in Paris; Jean-Luc in Drocourt; and the family waiting at the finish.
The point is not "strangers are kind" as a slogan. The point is that each act of
help changes the feasible next day of the route.

Tone and visual direction
-------------------------
Use a road-atlas / field-log visual language: warm paper, near-black type,
safety-orange route marks, compact date labels, and photography that feels like
evidence rather than tourism advertising. Keep slide copy concrete and sparse.
Avoid inspirational maxims, three-item slogan lists, generic travel lessons, and
AI-polished fragments. Speaker notes can carry the chronology and technical
detail; the screen should carry the images, numbers, and exact moments.
